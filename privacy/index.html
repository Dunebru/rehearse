Privacy Policy

Interview practice for the iPhone (bundle id com.vivekkesara.interviewpractice) · last updated 19 September 2026

Short version: everything you say and everything the app works out about it stays on your phone. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising, and no server that receives your voice, your words or your results. One optional feature sends something you type: if you ask for questions about a company, the company name and the role go to Google's Gemini to write them, and the app asks you first.

Your voice

The app uses the microphone only while you are answering a question. Your speech is turned into text on your own device, using Apple's on-device speech recognition. The app requires on-device transcription and will refuse to run rather than send your audio anywhere.

The app keeps a recording of each answer on your phone so you can hear it back. Recordings are kept for your last sixty answers, are excluded from your device's backups, and are deleted with the answer they belong to. Audio is never uploaded.

What is stored on your device

Your answers, meaning the question, the transcript, the timed sentences it was made of, the length, the pace, the pauses, the filler words and the date, are saved in a single file on your own device. The feedback you see is computed on the phone from that. None of it is uploaded or shared.

Your settings are stored alongside it: the track you chose, the interview date if you gave one, how you said you feel about it, your reminder time and your streak. These never leave the phone either. The file and the settings are included in your device's own iCloud or computer backup if you have that switched on, the same as any other app's data; the recordings are not.

Deleting the app deletes all of it. Settings also has "Delete all history", which removes every answer and every recording and keeps the app.

What the app fetches

On each launch the app reads two small files published at this address: one that can switch a broken feature off, and one that can carry new questions. They are the same files for everybody, nothing about you is sent with the request, and the host (GitHub Pages) sees what any web server sees, which is an IP address and the time.

The phone also downloads Apple's speech model once, from Apple. That download is what keeps transcription on the device.

Spiel reading your answer

This is optional and off until you turn it on, and the app shows a screen explaining it and asking first. With it on, three things go out after each answer: the question, the three things a complete answer to it covers, and the text of what you said that time. Your recording never goes: your voice stays on your phone. Your name, your streak and your other answers are not sent either.

The request goes to a server we run, which passes it to Google's Gemini under its Gemini API terms. What comes back is which parts of the question you covered and one thing to change. Neither we nor Google keep a record of it against you, and we do not store the transcript. The switch in Settings stops it immediately.

With it off, the app decides which parts you covered on the device, by looking for the phrases the question expects.

Questions for a company

This is optional, and the app shows a screen explaining it and asks for your agreement before the first request. When you ask for questions for a company, the app sends three things: the company or school name you typed, the role you typed, and which kind of interview you picked in the app. Nothing else is sent: not your voice, not your answers or transcripts, not your results, not your interview date, and no identifier for you or your phone.

The request goes to a server we run (a Supabase Edge Function), which passes those lines to Google's Gemini API. Gemini searches the web about the company and writes the questions, and they come back to your phone, where they are saved. Google processes the request under its Gemini API terms.

Our server keeps the questions it writes, filed under the company and the role they were written for, so that the next person asking about the same employer is served from that file instead of paying for another search. Nothing identifying you is stored beside them: no name, no account, no device identifier. To stop abuse it also keeps a count of requests per day against a scrambled (salted and hashed) form of the network address the request came from, which cannot be turned back into the address, and deletes it after two days. Like any web server, the hosting provider's short-lived operational logs may record that a request happened.

The app already contains questions for 134 common employers. Naming one of those sends nothing anywhere.

The questions are written by AI and can contain mistakes about a company. "Delete all history" removes them from your phone.

What the app does not do

No account and no sign-in. No analytics or crash reporting SDKs. No advertising and no advertising identifiers. No tracking across apps or websites. Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared except the company and role described above, when you ask for it.

The app never listens to a real interview and never supplies answers during one. It is for practice beforehand.

Purchases

The one-time unlock is handled entirely by Apple. Payment details are never seen by the app or by us; Apple tells the app only whether the unlock has been bought. Restore purchase in the app brings it back on a new phone.

Reminders

If you turn on the daily reminder, the notifications are scheduled on your phone by the app itself. No server sends them and nothing is registered anywhere.

Withdrawing permission

You can revoke access to the microphone or to speech recognition at any time in iOS Settings under Privacy & Security. The app will say it cannot run and will not try to work around it. Answers already on the phone are unaffected until you delete them.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone.
